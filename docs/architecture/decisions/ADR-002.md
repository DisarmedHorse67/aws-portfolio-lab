# ADR-002: Using three accounts for management, log-archive and workload

Status: accepted
Date: 29/10/26

Context: The project hosts a public website and runs security simulations
that create deliberately risky resources. Logs must stay trustworthy even
if the workload account is compromised, and organization-wide guardrails
(SCPs) do not apply to the management account. A second goal is to practice
the multi-account structure used in real cloud environments.

Options:
- Single account: simplest and cheapest to start; no separation between
  workloads, logs and billing, so one compromise or mistake affects everything.
- Two accounts (management and workload, logs kept in workload): some
  separation; logs can still be deleted by anyone who controls the workload account.
- Three accounts (management, log-archive, workload): clear separation of
  duties and tamper-resistant logs; more setup and a little more cost.
- AWS Control Tower with its standard accounts: automated setup; adds
  services with ongoing costs and hides details I want to learn.

Decision: Use three accounts under one AWS Organization: management for
Organizations, Identity Center, billing and SCPs (no workloads);
log-archive for CloudTrail and Flow Logs, with a policy that blocks
deletes; workload for the portfolio, alerts and labs. This limits the
damage of any single failure, keeps logs out of reach of simulated
attacks, and makes SCPs effective where the workloads run.

Consequences:
- Cost: accounts are free; GuardDuty is billed per account, and Free Tier
  plan benefits end for accounts in an Organization (see ADR-003).
- Effort: more setup (root MFA and an email address per account, provider
  aliases in Terraform, cross-account log delivery).
- Risk: a misconfigured cross-account policy can break log delivery; this
  is checked after setup by confirming log files arrive in the bucket.
- Benefit: the structure matches common cloud security practice and is
  visible in the repo.
