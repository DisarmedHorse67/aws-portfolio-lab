# ADR-003: Using the Paid-Plan for the project

Status: accepted
Date: 29/10/26

Context: The project is a public portfolio that must stay online well
beyond six months, and it uses an AWS Organization with three accounts
(see ADR-002). AWS's Free plan closes the account after 6 months or when
its credits run out, and joining or creating an Organization ends Free
plan benefits or upgrades the account to the Paid plan. The Free plan also
restricts some services. Monthly spending must stay at or under $5.

Options:
- Free plan only: no charges while it lasts, but the account closes after
  6 months, restricted services, and it cannot be combined with an Organization.
- Free plan first, upgrade later: keeps the credits and delays billing,
  but the Organization is created in an early phase, so the benefit lasts only a few days and adds a migration step.
- Paid plan from the start: pay-as-you-go with full service access and no automatic closure; real charges begin on the first day.

Decision: Use the Paid plan for every account. The Organization and the
need for a site that stays online make the Free plan unusable, and
upgrading later would add work for almost no benefit. Spending is
controlled by budgets and alerts instead of by plan limits.

Consequences:
- Cost: charges are real from day one, so the $5 cap relies on AWS
  Budgets alerts, Cost Anomaly Detection, resource tags and the lab reaper.
- Credits: any sign-up credits are a bonus, not part of the budget. They
  may not apply to accounts in an Organization and expire 12 months after
  account creation, so the plan does not depend on them. They also cannot
  pay for domain registration.
- Risk: a forgotten resource can now cost money;
